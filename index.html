01 / WHAT I DO

Big questions.
Small scale.

From individual cells to population cohorts, my work brings statistical genetics and functional genomics together to uncover the genes, cell types, and pathways driving complex disease.

01
✳

Single-cell genomics

Analysing population-scale scRNA-seq across tissues and cohorts to map cellular diversity and the transcriptional programmes reshaped in health and disease.

02
⌁

Statistical genetics & QTLs

Mapping cell-type-resolved expression QTLs and colocalising them with GWAS signals to connect genetic risk variants to effector genes in immune-mediated disease.

03
↗

Therapeutic insights

Integrating single-cell expression and genetic evidence within Open Targets to support systematic, evidence-backed drug target identification and prioritisation.

04 / ABOUT ME

Curiosity, code
& collaboration.

I joined EMBL-EBI’s Open Targets as its first dedicated single-cell analyst, integrating bulk, single-cell, proteomic, and QTL evidence into a revamped platform for therapeutic target prioritisation.

Originally a Londoner, I started in cellular biology at UCL (analysing yeast cellular ageing in the Bähler lab) — until a year abroad at the University of Waterloo introduced me to computer science. That led to an MSc in Bioinformatics at the University of Copenhagen (developing CELLECT-LDSC in the Pers Lab, alongside roles at Evaxion Biotech and Illumina) and a Wellcome Trust PhD in Mathematical Genomics and Medicine at the University of Cambridge and the Wellcome Sanger Institute.

In the Anderson Lab at Sanger, I co-led analyses for IBDverse, earning the Peter Landshoff Prize and the ESHG Lodewijk Sandkuijl Award. I’m also passionate about mentoring, running a campus-wide eQTL working group, and science communication — from Science Live KS3 lectures to a YourGenome YouTube video (>32,000 views).
